EXECUTIVE
SUMMARY
Young Ministers' Retreat 2025 was held from 26th to
30th December at RCCG Redemption City Camp,
under the convening leadership of Pastor Daniel
Olawande.
The five-day retreat brought together participants
from across Nigeria and International delegates in
what represents the largest edition of YMR to date,
with over 300,000 people attending physically
across the duration of the event.
The retreat maintained its established annual
structure; spanning prayer, worship, word sessions,
evangelism, children and teen programming,
counselling, and testimony documentation, while
introducing significant operational improvements
including
a
battalion-based
participant
classification model, a QR code-driven registration
system, and expanded multi-platform live
production coverage.
Key outcomes across the five days include 1,499
documented new converts, approximately 608,500
combined YouTube views across two channels,
over 5,000 attendees mobilised through the
Transportation Department, and 944 patients
treated across three medical stands with zero
deaths and zero referrals recorded.
Note on Data: Physical attendance is estimated at
over 300,000 based on field observations, as no
formal count was received from the Ushering
Department. All figures are based on verified
departmental submissions, with estimates or
pending data clearly indicated.

DEPARTMENTAL VOLUNTEER SUMMARY
YMR 2025 was powered by thousands of volunteers across more than 20 departments. The following
summarises performance and attendance by department based on submitted reports.
• Registration (207 assigned, 207 present): Full attendance maintained. 119% increase in data intake
driven by QR code system and improved team coordination.
• Live Production (30+ assigned, 30+ present): Full session coverage delivered across all five days
with no missed broadcasts.
• Photography (35 assigned, 27 present): Despite 8 absences, the team achieved 100% session
coverage.
• Counselling (220 assigned, 49 present): 22% attendance rate, yet all sessions were covered and
1,499 converts recorded without delays.
• Testimony (220 assigned, 49 present): All testimonies logged across sessions without interruption.
• Medical (1,505 registered, 215 present): 14% physical attendance. Despite this, 944 patients treated
across three stands with no fatalities or referrals.
• Logistics (546 assigned, ~45-50 active): Approximately 80% of departmental needs were met.
• Sanitation (153 assigned, 153 present): Full turnout. All facilities covered throughout.
• Children's Department (88 assigned, 88 present): Full attendance. 310 children served across the
retreat.
• Transportation (54 assigned, high attendance): 44 locations served; over 5,000 attendees
mobilised
• Volunteer Engagement (2,168 registered): 86 campuses onboarded across the programme.
• Music (90 assigned, 71 present): 40% increase in congregational engagement recorded.
• Hospitality (42 assigned, ~2 retained): All 14 listed guests served alongside unexpected international
delegates.
• Welfare (1,291 assigned, <100 present): Significant no-shows impacted market and welfare arm
coverage.
• PR (3 assigned, 2 present): Approximately 80% of targets met.
Pattern to Address: Multiple departments reported a significant gap between registered and physically
present volunteers. Medical (14%), Welfare, Testimony, and Counselling were most affected. MR 2026
must introduce a formal pre-event confirmation system beyond initial registration

IDENTIFIED GAPS AND CHALLENGES
• Incomplete departmental reporting: several departments missed the submission window, leaving
acknowledged gaps in the consolidated data.
• Physical attendance of over 300,000 was not formally documented. No crowd-count mechanism
was in place.
• Multiple departments lacked walkie-talkies, forcing reliance on personal mobile phones throughout
the event.
• Late disbursement of funds across Photography, Music, Live Production, PR, and Accommodation - a
recurring pattern.
• Live Production budget underfunded by 38.8% (N5M+ shortfall). Video switcher failure required
emergency resolution before Day 1.
• No medical personnel stationed in the Free Accommodation Arm despite repeated requests across
multiple years.
• Large gap between registered and physically present volunteers across several departments.
• Central Registration Hub relocation created electrical access issues on Day 1.
• Counselling area sound system was non-functional, forcing team relocation midevent.
• Children's Department faced hall space constraints, poor ventilation, and parents forcing entry
during sessions.
• Security coverage at the Welfare Market was absent at the scheduled time.
• Tag accountability was poor: 9 music tags, 7 security tags, and 24 Protocol Guest tags unaccounted
for.
• Bible Quiz on Days 3 and 4 communicated to Live Production only 10 minutes before it began.

YMR 2025 was a large-scale, spiritually purposeful gathering that demonstrated measurable progress in
operational maturity, media reach, and participant engagement. Over 300,000 people attended in
person, 1,499 new converts were documented, more than 608,000 combined YouTube views were
recorded, and 944 patients were treated with no fatalities. These figures represent a significant
milestone in the retreat's history.
What the 2025 edition also revealed is that operational infrastructure has not yet fully scaled to match
growth in attendance and complexity. The challenges identified in this report - funding delays, volunteer
attendance gaps, communication tool shortfalls, and data submission lapses - are systemic and
correctable. Each one has a named solution that is within reach before YMR 2026.
The groundwork laid this year is strong: the KPI-driven reporting framework, the battalion classification
system, the QR code registration model, and the expanded media documentation infrastructure all point
to an organisation that is building deliberately. The work for the coming year is not to redesign these
systems but to resource them properly, formalise the processes around them, and follow through on
recommendations that have, in several cases, been submitted more than once. YMR 2026 has the
conditions to serve a larger gathering with greater operational confidence, measurably stronger impact
documentation, and a participant experience that reflects the scale and seriousness of what this retreat
has become.
