EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The 2024 edition of the Young Ministers Retreat (YMR) was held from December 26
to 30, 2024, at the Old Auditorium, Redemption City, along the Lagos-Ibadan
Expressway. Themed "Floodgates", this year's retreat drew an estimated 281,003
participants from across Nigeria and around the world, both on-site and online.
Across all livestream sessions, the retreat recorded a combined 294,506 online views,
further extending YMR's global reach and impact.
The retreat opened with Volunteers' Night on December 26, a time to spiritually
prepare and align all serving teams. From December 27 to 29, participants engaged
in marathon prayer sessions, deep worship, Bible study, impactful word
ministrations, and strategic moments of impartation. The event concluded on
December 30 with thanksgiving and street evangelism, sending participants back to
their communities with renewed vision and fire.
Operationally, YMR 2024 was powered by thousands of volunteers across multiple
departments, including Programs, Protocol, Logistics, Registration, Publicity,
Tech/Media, Transport, Accommodation, Prayer, Hospitality, Security, and more. The
hybrid format extended the retreat's reach globally through platforms such as Dove
TV, YouTube, MixIr, Facebook and Instagram Live.
The scale of this year's event brought remarkable achievements, including a
record-breaking attendance, strong online engagement, and sustained intercessory
coverage throughout the retreat. However, it also revealed operational challenges
that provide clear areas for improvement. These included volunteer duplication
across departments, delays in transport and
accommodation readiness,
communication gaps between certain teams, technical disruptions to screen and
audio systems, and crowd flow management issues during peak times.
This report provides a comprehensive review of YMR 2024, including planning and
coordination processes, program execution, attendance analysis, departmental
performance, challenges, lessons learned, and strategic recommendations. It serves
both as a record of the retreat's impact and a roadmap for delivering an even more
seamless and impactful experience for the projected 1 million participants expected
at YMR 2025.

PROGRAM OBJECTIVES & KPIs
The Young Ministers Retreat (YMR) 2024 was planned with specific spiritual and
operational objectives to ensure the mission of the retreat was achieved while
maintaining high standards in event delivery. The main objectives were:
Spiritual Impact - Create an atmosphere for divine encounters through extended
prayer, deep worship, and the teaching of God's Word.
Ministerial Equipping - Train and equip participants with biblical principles,
ministerial discipline, and marketplace relevance.
Global Reach - Extend YMR's influence beyond the physical venue through
livestreaming, online engagement, and international participation.
Volunteer Empowerment - Mobilize, train, and deploy a large volunteer workforce
capable of managing the retreat's growing attendance.
Operational Excellence - Ensure seamless coordination across all departments for a
spiritually enriching and logistically smooth retreat.
Performance for each department was measured against defined Key Performance
Indicators (KPls). These KPIs are documented in the YMR 2024 KPI Document, which
should be used as the reference for reviewing results and measuring impact.
The document contains the agreed performance metrics for all operational areas
including Programs, Protocol, Logistics, Publicity, Tech/Media, Transport,
Registration, Accommodation, Prayer, Hospitality, and Security.

PLANNING AND COORDINATION REVIEW
The planning and coordination of YMR 2024 began months in advance and involved
multiple layers of stakeholder engagement, departmental setup, and operational
oversight. As the scale of the retreat continued to grow, the planning team prioritized
early structure, clarity in responsibilities, and increased interdepartmental
collaboration.
Planning Timeline and Milestones
Preparations began in earnest by mid-year with departmental HODs onboarded
progressively. The planning timeline followed key milestones including:
i. Strategy meetings with core leaders and directors
li. Departmental visioning sessions
ili. KPI development for all departments
iv. Volunteer link release and onboarding (ahead of December)
v. PR and social media campaign rollout
vi. Tech, registration, logistics, and accommodation preparation
vii. Final rehearsals and departmental readiness check
While early planning started strong, there were delays in final approvals, floor
mapping, and budget confirmations for some departments, which affected
timelines and execution in certain areas.
Team Structure and Departmental Coordination
YMR 2024 operated with a decentralized leadership model led by the Project
Manager and supported by departmental Heads of Departments (HODs). Each
department functioned semi-autonomously, with specific Pls guiding execution.
The structure covered:
- Core Teams: Programs, Protocol, Registration, Logistics, Publicity, Tech/Media,
Transport, Accommodation, Volunteers, Prayer, Hospitality, and Security.
- Support & Specialized Units: Campus Mobilization, Welfare, Medical, Live
Production, Bible Study, Call Centre, Counselling & Follow-Up, Children, Testimonies,
Music, and Traffic Control.
Regular leadership meetings and weekly updates helped monitor progress, resolve
issues, and maintain momentum across teams.

next morning's activities.

CHALLENGES ENCOUNTERED
While YMR 2024 was impactful and well-attended, several operational and logistical
challenges affected the smooth flow of the retreat. One recurring issue was
volunteer duplication across multiple departments, which led to double-counting
and uneven manpower distribution. Some departments had more volunteers than
needed, while others faced shortages during critical moments, especially during
high-traffic sessions.
Interdepartmental communication gaps created delays in sharing urgent
information. This was particularly evident during high-movement sessions such as
the prayer walk, where Security, Protocol, and Media needed instant coordination
but received updates too slowly. The YMR Experience Team also operated with
minimal communication with the Project Office, which caused overlaps and
confusion in activity execution.
Hospitality faced significant pressure managing a crowded green room. At various
points, the space exceeded its intended capacity due to unclear access control,
leading to discomfort for guest ministers and VIPs. In some cases, the absence of a
designated holding area for other visitors meant they were redirected into the green
room, further straining the space.
Technical and media operations have experienced disruptions. Several large screens
positioned after the 5J section in the auditorium were non-functional during key
sessions, limiting visibility for participants seated in those areas. Audio glitches
occurred during some worship and teaching moments, and projection delays
disrupted the timing of song lyrics and scripture displays.
Accommodation logistics were stretched due to record attendance. The paid
accommodation process suffered from late communication of policies,
inconsistencies in early check-in coordination, and readiness delays for reserved
rooms. Free accommodation facilities experienced long queues before additional
hostels were opened, causing frustration among participants.
Transportation delays were linked to late approvals for bus hire and driver
arrangements, leaving insufficient preparation time. This impacted pick-up
schedules for participants and slowed the movement of Hospitality teams attending
to guest ministers at different lodges.

CHALLENGES RECORDED
While YMR 2024 was a success in its execution and spiritual impact, the scale and
complexity of the retreat revealed several operational and coordination challenges
that provide learning opportunities for future editions. These challenges cut across
planning, execution, and post-event follow-up.
Volunteer Allocation and Duplication
One of the most persistent issues was multiple sign-ups by the same volunteer
across different departments, leading to double-counting and uneven manpower
distribution. Some departments experienced an excess of volunteers, while others
struggled with understaffing during peak periods.
Interdepartmental Coordination Gaps
Although interdepartmental synergy improved compared to previous editions, there
were still moments where communication lagged. Examples include:
i. Delays in passing urgent information between Security, Protocol, and Media during
the prayer walk.
ii. Last-minute adjustments in floor mapping that were not fully cascaded to affected
departments.
ili. Inconsistent updates on schedule changes, impacting stage transitions and
logistics readiness.
Technical and Equipment Limitations
i. Certain departments faced constraints due to equipment shortages or late
procurement: Limited availability of walkie-talkies for high-movement departments
such as Logistics, Security, and Transport.
ii. Occasional audio glitches and screen delays during worship sessions and some
plenaries.
ili. Insufficient backup systems for critical tech functions.
Late Approvals and Finalizations
Final decisions on some program elements, floor mapping, and departmental
requests came later than planned, creating last-minute pressure for execution
teams. This impacted:
i. Stage layout readiness.
ii. Guest seating arrangements.
ili. Logistics and rental follow-up timelines.

Accommodation and Transport Strain
On the accommodation side, both the free and paid options experienced challenges.
The demand for paid accommodation surged far beyond initial projections, with
many participants seeking earlier check-in due to travel schedules and
departmental engagements.
While the Paid Accommodation team worked to manage requests, late
communication of policies and processes meant some participants were unaware of
check-in procedures and timelines. Coordination with departments requiring early
check-in for their members was inconsistent, leading to last-minute adjustments. In
some cases, rooms reserved for paid bookings were not ready on arrival, which
caused delays in settling participants. The absence of a consolidated early check-in
accommodation policy meant that the team had to make exceptions on the spot,
slowing down the entire process.
The Free Accommodation team also faced strain in allocating beds due to the high
influx of attendees, particularly during peak arrival hours on December 26. Also, an
issue with respect to the sanitation of the hostel came up and the leaders in-charge
locked the people staying in the hostels to clean up resulting to some participants
missing scheduled transportation.

Transportation delays were another major concern. While the Transport team had
developed a comprehensive route and movement plan, late approvals for certain
operational requests - including bus hires and driver arrangements - reduced the
preparation window. This led to delayed dispatch of buses from pick-up points,
longer-than-expected waiting times for volunteers and Hospitality teams, and
reduced flexibility for quick-response movements when last-minute program
changes arose.
Crowd Flow and Venue Navigation
During YMR 2024, peak entry and exit times caused congestion at key access points,
slowing participant movement despite the efforts of Security and Crowd Control
teams. The absence of clear directional signage made navigation difficult for
first-time attendees and international delegates, leading to heavy reliance on verbal
guidance from volunteers. Inside the auditorium, last-minute seat changes and
crowd surges disrupted seating arrangements, while limited pre-session dispersal
strategies prolonged transitions between sessions. Although there were no major
security incidents, these challenges highlighted the need for improved signage,
proactive movement planning, and more structured crowd management strategies
for future events.
Real-Time Problem Escalation
YMR 2024 used departmental WhatsApp groups and central broadcast lists for
communication, but these were not always effective for urgent, on-ground issues. In
high-pressure moments such as technical glitches, sudden program changes, or
crowd control needs, relying on text messages delayed responses.
Departments like Security, Protocol, Logistics, and Transport were most affected.
Without enough two-way radios or a dedicated rapid response system, urgent
matters often went through to several people before reaching the right
decision-maker. This caused time lags in resolving issues such as adjusting seating
for VIPs, rerouting transport, or fixing technical problems during live sessions.
The absence of a single incident command structure also meant that some quick
decisions were made without considering their effect on other teams. While most
challenges were resolved through the dedication of departmental leads, a faster and
more centralized escalation process is needed for future events.

LESSONS LEARNED
MR 2024 reinforced the strengths of the retreat while highlighting areas for growth
in planning, coordination, and execution. The following key lessons emerged from
the event:
i. Early Decision-Making Improves Efficiency: Approvals for critical areas such as
transportation, accommodation, and stage mapping need to be finalized well ahead
of the retreat to prevent last-minute pressure on departments.
ii. Clear Volunteer Allocation Prevents Overlaps: A centralized volunteer
management system is essential to avoid multiple sign-ups across departments,
ensure balanced manpower, and reduce confusion on the ground.
ill. Structured Communication Tools Are Critical: While WhatsApp groups worked for
general
updates, urgent operational issues require a dedicated real-time
communication system, such as two-way radios or a central command channel.
iv. Proactive Crowd Flow Planning Reduces Congestion: Advance planning for entry
and exit strategies, coupled with visible signage and designated walking lanes, can
significantly improve movement during peak times.
V. Cross-Department Collaboration Strengthens Execution: Regular pre-event joint
sessions between related departments such as Protocol, Logistics, Security, and
Transport help align responsibilities and minimize operational clashes.
vi. Follow-Up Systems Drive Post-Event Impact: A structured approach to collecting,
verifying, and analyzing testimonies and feedback enhances engagement and
provides actionable insights for future events.
vil. Retention of Experienced Volunteers Builds Consistency: Keeping trained
volunteers engaged year-round through regular communication, recognition, and
refresher training strengthens team readiness for the next retreat.

PROPOSED ACTION PLAN FOR NEXT YMR
With YMR's growth trajectory pointing towards a projected 1 million participants in
the next edition, planning, resource allocation, and execution strategies must be
significantly scaled. This action plan outlines key steps to ensure a seamless
experience for both on-site and online participants while maintaining the spiritual
depth and excellence YMR is known for.
Early Planning and Decision-Making
i. Launch the YMR planning process at least nine months in advance to allow
sufficient time for large-scale preparation.
il. Secure early approvals for transportation contracts, accommodation bookings,
stage designs, and equipment rentals.
ill. Lock in the event theme, guest minister list, and main program flow by mid-year
to guide publicity and logistics.
Volunteer Management at Scale
i. Implement a centralized volunteer registration and allocation system that
automatically prevents multiple sign-ups and balances manpower distribution.
ii. Target recruitment of at least 15,000 trained volunteers across all departments,
including overflow standby teams.
iii. Introduce mandatory orientation and training sessions for all volunteers, with
department-specific drills for high-demand units such as Security, Logistics,
Transport, and Tech.
iv. Develop a Volunteer Retention Program to keep experienced members engaged
year-round through communication, skill development, and recognition.
Infrastructure and Venue Readiness
i. Map and prepare expanded seating and overflow zones, including outdoor viewing
centers with large LED screens.
ii. Design crowd flow lanes with clear entry, exit, and emergency routes to handle
high participant volume.
iii. Increase directional signage across Redemption City to guide international
delegates and first-time attendees.
iv. Allocate dedicated shuttle routes within the venue for quick movement between
accommodation blocks, the auditorium, and other key locations.
